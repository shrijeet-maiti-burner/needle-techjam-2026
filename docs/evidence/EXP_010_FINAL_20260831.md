## What changed since 30 August

The previous run (`20260830T050643Z`, agent at 0.878039) failed 8 gates. This run fails 6, and the *character* of the failures changed completely:

| | 30 Aug | 31 Aug |
|---|---|---|
| meaning-preserving HR breaches (limit −0.02) | 4 | **0** |
| worst preserving ΔHR | −0.11 (accents) | −0.010 |
| slices fully clean | 6 | **10** |

`accents` (−0.11) and `filler` (−0.035) are now **0.000 across every metric**. Neither was fixed by a robustness change: the emission and promotion work in #14/#19/#21 raised baseline recall to 1.000, and a target that is reliably retrieved is a target that survives perturbation. The `filler` result supersedes my earlier recommendation to accept that slice as unfixable — it was unfixable *by the means I had considered*, which is not the same claim, and I was wrong to frame it as broadly as I did.

Every remaining failure is `target_removal_rate > 0.0` alone. No meaning-preserving HR drop is within a factor of two of the gate.

## The remaining meaning-preserving misses

Three distinct sessions across `paraphrase` and `word_order`; `public_0020` fails in both. All three lost the target from the candidate pool, so these are retrieval misses, not ranking misses.

Isolated by ablation:

| session | slice | no promotion | no identification | pure sparse |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| public_0007 | word_order | miss | miss | **hit** |
| public_0100 | paraphrase | miss | **hit** | **hit** |
| public_0020 | word_order, paraphrase | miss | miss | miss |

**public_0020 is not recoverable by any configuration tried.** Its target card carries a single 90-character fabric string (`solid colors 100 cotton heather grey 90 cotton 10 polyester ...`); shuffling its internal clauses destroys the discriminating text for every retrieval path at once.

The other two are the cost of a deliberate trade. `adaptive_slate` with `early_slate_size=1` emits one product per early turn, and the slate only tops up from sparse when it is short of `output_limit`. When promotion returns one confident-but-wrong candidate, `len(recommendation_ids) == output_limit == 1` and the sparse fallback never runs, so the turn is spent. Pure sparse recovers both because it has no early slate at all.

## Fix considered and rejected

Widening the early slate is the only lever that reaches these sessions. Measured on the released set:

| | score | HR@10 | MRR | MTTC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `early_slate_size=1` (shipped) | **0.978500** | 1.000 | 0.996667 | 2.025 |
| `early_slate_size=2` | 0.958450 | 1.000 | 0.917500 | 1.840 |
| `early_slate_size=3` | 0.946250 | 1.000 | 0.870833 | 1.750 |

**−0.0201 clean score to chase two sessions in two perturbation slices.** HR@10 is already saturated at 1.000, so a wider early slate buys no clean recall and pays for it in MRR. Rejected.

An order-insensitive bucket key was also considered and dropped before implementation. `_card_lookup_key` already supports `unordered=True`, and it is deliberately gated to post-override turns (`allow_ordered = intent_version == 1`) with a documented reason: before four disclosure positions, a partial prefix of the real target can equal the complete set of a shorter card. Independently, clause-level fragments already absorb most reordering — measured over 2000 real multi-clause catalog values, a shuffled restatement still matches an indexed signature **84.6%** of the time, and **95.3%** via a clause fragment. The residual is not where the losses are.

## Recommendation on the zero gate

`max_target_removal_rate` is preregistered at zero and is the only thing now failing. Three of the six failures — `attribute_swap`, `constraint_drop`, `negation` — are *meaning-changing* slices, where removing the original target is arguably the correct response to a customer who changed what they asked for. Reading those as defects makes the gate report a success as a failure.

I am not relaxing a preregistered gate on freeze day, and I am not proposing the numbers be reinterpreted after seeing them. Recorded for the post-competition write-up: the gate should have been specified per meaning class.

### The negation slice moved, and it is not a regression

`negation` target removal went **0.006410 → 0.044872**, and its ΔHR from −0.005
to −0.035. That is the only number in the matrix that moved the wrong way, so it
was chased to the session level.

The perturbation prefixes a disclosed constraint with `no ` or `non-`. All seven
newly-missed sessions are that shape, six of them on a *hard* constraint. The
agent now correctly excludes the negated attribute, so it stops recommending the
product that carries it — and the evaluator still scores against the original
target. **Correct negation handling is scored as target removal here.** An agent
that kept the target would be ignoring the customer.

This is the meaning-class problem this record already flagged, now with a
concrete instance: three of the failing gates are meaning-*changing* slices where
removal is the desired behaviour, and the preregistered zero-removal gate reads
that success as a failure.

Two documents currently disagree with the shipped runtime and should be read with
this section: `EXP_025.md` tabulates negation at 0.006410, and
`REDTEAM_HUMAN_STATE.md` reports the matrix as "identical except
meaning-changing negation MTTC" — true for its own narrow before/after, but not
across `4e36df6 → 8a9c9b7`. Neither is wrong about what it measured; both predate
this value.

**No transfer regression.** The −0.000100 on one panel reproduced exactly on an

margins these panels were used to decide.

Worth stating plainly for the submission narrative: the released set is
saturated at HR@10 1.000, and these panels are not (1.000 / 0.990 / 0.985).
**0.9785 is a ceiling, not an expectation.** The 0.962–0.979 panel range is the
better guide to unseen behaviour, and nothing in our documentation should imply
otherwise.
