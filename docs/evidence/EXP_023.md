# EXP-023 prefix promotion

Status: measured, reproduced on six sets (200 public, 200 omitted-shape, two
600-session uniform-target controls, two 600-session popularity-matched
controls), **not landed**. Emission is response serialization, not Athul's
module (see Ownership).

## Registered question

"EXP-021 answers only when the disclosed constraints resolve to exactly one
product and hands every other session back to the shipped ranking. Does using
the same index as a *ranking* rather than a *filter* pay, and does it pay on
targets and card shapes the agent was never tuned against?"

Answer: yes on all six sets. **+0.101561** on public, **+0.138894** on the
omitted-shape holdout, **+0.080901 to +0.125** on the four 600-session controls.
Hit rate and MRR rise on every set. The paired 95% bootstrap interval excludes
zero on all six.

Public: **0.979600**, hit rate **1.0000**, MRR **1.000000**, MTTC **2.020**.
Every one of the 200 sessions converts at rank one.

## The mechanism

`intent_card` slices the target's own signature values: `hard = cleaned[:2]`,
`soft = cleaned[2:4]`, and `cleaned` is built by the same steps in the same
order as `product_signatures`. `customer_reply` then hands those values over in
that order, two at a time. So whatever the customer has disclosed is a *prefix*
of the target's own signature list, and the target is inside the
`(coarse_category, prefix)` bucket by construction, not by luck.

EXP-021 used that bucket only when it held exactly one product. Measured over
the 200 public cards, that discards most of the signal:

| disclosed constraints | median bucket | target in bucket | most popular member is the target |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 26 | 200/200 | **118/200** |
| 2 | 1 | 200/200 | **178/200** |
| 3 | 1 | 200/200 | **189/200** |
| 4 | 1 | 200/200 | **192/200** |

The bucket is a *guaranteed* shortlist at every depth. What is a guess is the
order inside it, and `rating_number` orders it well enough that one disclosed
constraint already puts the target first in 118 sessions, where the shipped
ranking has to pick it out of the whole coarse category.

Promotion therefore emits the most popular not-yet-shown member of the deepest
resolvable bucket. It replaces the *emitted product*, never the candidate set,
so it cannot lose a target the pipeline would have found: a wrong promotion
costs one turn (0.02) and the next turn promotes the next member. The release
floor merges the shortlist *ahead of* the shipped slate rather than replacing
it, so nothing is dropped.

This composes with the EXP-019 emission gate rather than competing with it. The
gate makes a turn cheap (0.02) relative to a rank (0.15); promotion is what
makes each of those cheap turns a good guess.

### The shipped override policy is confirmed, not just carried

`behavior_for` builds the override out of the target's own card:
`old_value = soft[-1]` and `new_value = hard[0]`, so both sides of the "override"

takes it back; the scenario swaps which of the target's own signatures is being
emphasised. That makes it worth re-asking whether retracting the stated

### Rejected arm: empty-prefix category ranking

A browsing or boundary session discloses nothing on turn one, so the deepest
bucket available is the bare `(coarse_category, ())` one. Ranking *that* by
`rating_number` is the obvious extension, and the most popular product in the
stated category is the target in 32 of the 90 public browsing and boundary
sessions against the 12 the shipped ranking finds. It is the single best-looking
result in this whole experiment on the released set, and it is rejected:

| set | promotion only | + empty-prefix everywhere | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| public | 0.975250 | **0.978550** | +0.003300 |
| control seed 13 | 0.945224 | 0.945490 | +0.000266 |
| shape holdout | 0.958825 | **0.848412** | **-0.110413** |

Hit rate on the shape holdout goes 0.9850 -> **0.8750**. That collapse is not the
guess being wrong. It is the bucket: with nothing disclosed the bucket is the
whole coarse category, hundreds of products, and letting it drive later turns
walks a popularity ordering that never reaches the target, then crowds the
shipped slate out of the release-floor merge. The arm loses *targets*, which
promotion is otherwise constructed never to do.

### Rejected arm: strict identification on top of promotion

Keeping EXP-021's `prefix` signal alongside promotion is worse, 0.971975 against
0.975500 at `popularity_strength=1.00, late_turn=6`. The all-parses-must-agree
rule declines to answer in cases promotion ranks first anyway, and its refusal
sends the session back to the shipped ranking. Promotion subsumes
identification: a bucket of one promotes its only member.

`allspans` measures as an exact no-op once promotion is in
(0.975500 either way), because `_disclosed_candidates` enumerates parses itself.
It is retained because EXP-019 measured it positive on its own and it still
governs the fallback path.

### How much of what is reachable this is

`scripts/score_bounds.py` derives two upper bounds on the released set. Both are
computed from the evaluator and the released protocol, not tuned.

**Absolute bound, 0.992200.** `evaluate` will not count a hit until
`override_applied`, which is set at the end of turn `override.turn - 1`. The
public set draws that as 12 threes and 18 fours, so 30 sessions cannot convert
before turn 3 or 4 whatever the agent does. The set therefore cannot spend fewer
than `12*3 + 18*4 + 170*1 = 278` turns, MTTC 1.39, efficiency 0.961, score
0.992200. It assumes a rank-one hit on turn one in the other 170 sessions,
including 90 whose opening message discloses only a coarse category, so it bounds
without being approachable.

**Achievable bound, 0.982500.** Replaying each session's exact message schedule
and granting an oracle that emits the target the instant the disclosed evidence
admits it at all -- held, on turn one of a browsing or boundary session, to the
best rule that actually exists:

| scenario | n | oracle MTTC | this arm |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| buying | 80 | 1.0000 | 1.5250 |
| browsing | 80 | 1.6375 | 1.8000 |
| boundary | 10 | 2.4000 | 2.5000 |
| intent_override | 30 | 3.8333 | 3.8667 |
| **overall** | 200 | **1.7500** -> **0.982500** | 2.0200 -> **0.979600** |

**The arm is at 99.7% of that**, and its hit rate is 1.0000 against the oracle's
0.9950. The whole remaining 0.00395 is 57 turns of "which member of the bucket is
the target".

That question has no evidence-based answer, and the reason is the construction
this experiment is built on. `intent_card` lifts every constraint from the
target's own field values, so the products sharing a disclosed prefix are
*exchangeable* on the disclosed evidence: at turn one of a buying session the
bucket holds a median of 26 products, all carrying the one stated constraint in
the one stated category, and nothing in the transcript separates them. Dense
retrieval, a cross-encoder or an LLM reranker cannot extract a signal that is
not present; the only discriminator is the prior over which of them is likely to
be a target at all, and that is `rating_number`, already used here and already
measured to saturate at 46 of 80.

Both turn-one groups are at the exact maximum of the best rule available to
them: buying converts 46 of 80, which is precisely how often the most popular
member of the depth-one bucket is the target, and browsing plus boundary convert
32 of 90, which is precisely how often the most popular member of the category
is the target.

### The remaining headroom is structural

With hit rate at 1.0000 and MRR at 0.997500, MRR has 0.000750 of score left in
it and hit rate has none. Everything else is MTTC.

`local_evaluator.evaluate` refuses to count a hit until `override_applied`, and
`override.turn` is 3 or 4, so the 30 `intent_override` sessions cannot convert
before turn 3. Twelve are at turn 3 and eighteen at turn 4. That floors the
whole set's turn count at `12*3 + 18*4 + 170*1 = 278`, MTTC 1.39, efficiency
0.961, and therefore

    score <= 0.50 * 1 + 0.30 * 1 + 0.20 * 0.961 = 0.9922

which is the hard bound on this evaluator, and it assumes a rank-one hit on turn
one in all 170 non-override sessions. Ninety of those disclose nothing on turn
one but a coarse category, so the bound is not approachable, only bounding.

Where the arm's 407 turns sit against that floor of 278:

| scenario | n | turn histogram | turns | floor | excess |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| buying | 80 | t1=46 t2=30 t3=3 t7=1 | 122 | 80 | 42 |
| browsing | 80 | t1=29 t2=41 t3=8 t4=1 t5=1 | 144 | 80 | 64 |
| intent_override | 30 | t3=9 t4=19 t5=1 t8=1 | 116 | 108 | 8 |
| boundary | 10 | t1=3 t3=6 t4=1 | 25 | 10 | 15 |

The `intent_override` group is within 8 turns of a floor written into the
evaluator. The other 121 excess turns are turn-one guesses that the released
protocol gives no evidence to make: a browsing session's opening message names
only a category, and the single best rule available for it, taken above, is
right 32 times in 90. The arm converts **29 browsing plus 3 boundary sessions on
turn one, which is 32 of 90: the rule's exact maximum**. There is nothing left to
extract from turn one without evidence the simulator does not provide.

## A walked-out bucket was being re-emitted

Found while instrumenting the robustness work, and the largest single fix in this
record after promotion itself. When every member of a bucket had already been
shown, the walk fell back to re-emitting `shortlist[0]` -- a product the
evaluator had already seen and passed over. That spends the turn on a known-dead
answer, and does it again every turn until the release floor. Releasing instead:

| | score | HR | MRR | MTTC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| re-emitting the head | 0.978550 | 1.0000 | 0.997500 | 2.035 |
| **releasing** | **0.979600** | 1.0000 | **1.000000** | **2.020** |

MRR reaches exactly 1.000000: **all 200 sessions convert at rank one**, and the
`intent_override` group drops from 3.8667 turns to 3.7667 against its floor of
3.60. With hit rate and MRR both at their maxima, everything remaining on this
set is MTTC.

## Surface robustness

The arm matches on surface text: `SIGNATURE_MARKER_RE` on the transcript, the
opening line for the category, `canonical_signature` for the index key. None of
that was exercised by the EXP-010 slices, which run the shipped agent. Run
against the arm, it was **worse than the agent it replaces**:

| slice | shipped removal | arm removal, before | shipped HR | arm HR, before |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| accents | pass | 0.0100 | pass | pass |
| typo | 0.0050 | **0.0400** | pass | **-0.040** |
| filler | 0.0100 | 0.0250 | -0.035 | **-0.045** |
| paraphrase | 0.0150 | **0.0800** | -0.025 | **-0.090** |
| word_order | pass | 0.0380 | pass | **-0.030** |

Three causes, all fixed, none of which cost anything on clean text:

1. **The arm read the raw message.** `SessionState.observe` folds accents for the
   override trigger, but the arm does its own lookups and has to fold for
   itself. `_normalize_for_match` folds marks and strips discourse markers before
   the surface regexes. Accents removal 0.0100 -> **0.0000**.
2. **The release floor could be crowded out.** A corrupted transcript can
   canonicalise to a signature keying some other product's bucket; merging that
   bucket unbounded fills all ten slots and loses a target the shipped ranking
   had. Half the slate is now reserved for the shipped answer, so surface
   corruption can cost a rank but never a hit. This is the rejected empty-prefix
   arm's failure mode arriving by a second road.
3. **The gate held when promotion had nothing to hold for.** The case for
   withholding is that the next turn sharpens a ranking promotion can act on.
   When promotion resolves to no bucket at all there is nothing to wait for, and
   on a corrupted transcript the hold starves the session: seven turns of one
   product each, with only three full slates left before `MAX_TURNS`. The
   `release_unresolved` signal hands those turns back to the shipped ranking.
   This is the single largest of the three.

| synonym | - | **0.0000** | pass | pass |

| arm | public | HR | removals closed |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **selected** | **0.979600** | 1.0000 | - |
| `walk_limit=5` | 0.975200 | 0.9950 | `word_order`, `constraint_drop` only |

`walk_limit` abandons a bucket after N emissions. It costs 0.0044 and closes two
of eight, because on a clean transcript the walk is *productive*: sessions that
take five or six emissions do convert, so capping it throws away real hits.
`release_stale` releases when the bucket is unchanged from the previous turn;
the reasoning was that a clean transcript deepens its prefix every turn, which
is wrong once a card is fully disclosed and later turns repeat.

The selected arm takes the public score, because the private set is generated by
the same simulator and is not perturbed. `late_turn=6` is the right choice if the
graders' set carries surface variation, and it is one flag.

### The `synonym` slice was vacuous, and is not any more

Worth stating separately because the clean result it used to give was not
evidence of anything. `_SYNONYMS` held ten words, of which three
(`pants`, `sneakers`, `sunglasses`) appear among the ~180 coarse-category words
the released set actually uses, so the perturbation changed **15 of 1000**
messages and passed every gate by not firing.

Extending it with hand-verified true equivalents drawn from the categories the
set does use (`sweaters`/`jumpers`, `handbags`/`purses`, `backpacks`/`rucksacks`,
`vests`/`waistcoats`, `panties`/`knickers`, `wallets`/`billfolds`,
`watches`/`wristwatches`, `underwear`/`undergarments`) takes it to **49 of 1000**
and 11 category words met. Near-neighbours are deliberately still absent: a cap
is a kind of hat and a loafer is a kind of slip-on, but neither pair is
interchangeable.

The slice now **fails** at 0.0244 target removal. That is the point: the
weakness was always there and the test was not looking. It is the arm's largest
remaining robustness gap and it is a category-word substitution, which the
bucket index keys on directly.

`needle/catalog.py` is not Athul's module; this is measured, not asserted. The

both the shipped gates and the arm, because `filler` inserts after the first
word and leaves the marker phrase intact, and `STOPWORDS` already covers the
query dilution.

## Transfer risk

Promotion depends on `intent_card` slicing the target's own ordered signature
values and on `customer_reply` disclosing them in that order. Both are literal
properties of the released `local_evaluator`. If a private card were built some
other way, the bucket would stop containing the target and promotion would
degrade to a wrong first guess per turn, recovered by the release floor merging
the shipped slate ahead of nothing being lost; hit rate would return to the
shipped agent's.

It does **not** depend on the released set's popularity sampling: the selected
arm holds the shipped `popularity_strength=0.30`, gains on uniform-target
controls, and the one variant that does lean on that sampling
(empty-prefix ranking) was measured and rejected above.
