# EXP-022 popularity, re-measured on targets that look like targets

Status: measured. **Corrects EXP-016**, whose recommendation was drawn from an
instrument that could not answer the question it was asked.

## What EXP-016 got wrong

EXP-016 measured `popularity_strength` on the omitted-shape holdout and a
600-session control, found it negative on both, and recommended lowering it. The
measurement was sound. The sets were not.

| set | n | median target `rating_number` | has price |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| public | 200 | **6846** | 0.890 |
| shape holdout | 200 | 3 | 0.075 |
| control seed13 | 600 | 12 | 0.188 |
| whole catalog | 50000 | 12 | 0.211 |

Both holdouts draw targets close to uniformly from the catalog. Public targets
are roughly 500x more reviewed and carry a price four times as often. That gap
is not a public-set quirk to be corrected for: targets are **real purchase
records**, and purchased products are popular and well described, so the private
targets are drawn from the same kind of population. The competition
specification says as much - "the hidden target is based on a real purchase
record".

Judging a popularity prior against uniformly sampled targets therefore measures
a harder task than the one being scored, and rejects the prior whether or not it
transfers. Khanna-Aman reached the same conclusion independently on their own
submission and said it plainly: "sampling uniformly would have measured a
harder task, not a generalisation gap". They built a popularity-decile-matched
set for exactly this reason. We did not, and drew the wrong conclusion.

## The matched instrument

`scripts/build_matched_holdout.py` samples targets to match the public targets'
`rating_number` decile distribution, keeps the released scenario mix, requires
the eligibility properties every public target has (features and details
present), and excludes every public target.

    median rating_number   reference 6846   drawn 7010 (seed 13) / 7032 (seed 29)
    disjoint from public   yes

Price presence reaches 0.60 against the reference 0.89, so the match is on
popularity rather than on every property at once. That is a limit of this set,
recorded here rather than smoothed over.

## Result

`popularity_strength` 0.30 to 1.00, under the EXP-021 arm, 1600 held-out
sessions across two independent seeds:

| set | n | pop 0.30 | pop 1.00 | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| public | 200 | 0.950606 | **0.959433** | +0.008827 |
| matched, seed 13 | 600 | 0.933967 | **0.950720** | **+0.016753** |
| matched, seed 29 | 600 | 0.928796 | **0.943875** | **+0.015079** |
| uniform control, seed 13 | 600 | 0.934080 | 0.932296 | -0.001784 |

On held-out targets drawn like real ones, the stronger prior gains **more** than
it does on public, not less. The only set that rejects it is the one whose
targets look nothing like either split.

This is the opposite of the usual overfitting signature. A public-set artefact
gains on public and loses on held-out data; this gains twice as much on held-out
data as on public, on two independent seeds, and loses only where the target
population was constructed wrongly.

## What this does not establish

- The matched set is still our own construction. It matches the popularity
  decile profile and the eligibility filter, not the organizer's sampler.
- Price presence is matched only to 0.60 against 0.89.
- If the private split were drawn uniformly from the catalog rather than from
  purchase records, the uniform control would be the right instrument and this
  conclusion would invert. The specification's "real purchase record" wording is
  the evidence against that, and it is wording, not a guarantee.
- `popularity_strength` is capped at 1.0 by `CatalogIndex`. Whether the optimum
  lies above the cap is unmeasured.

## Recommendation

Withdraw the EXP-016 recommendation to lower `popularity_strength`. Raise it to
1.00, on this evidence, and retire the uniform control sets as a decision
instrument for anything that depends on the target population. The shape holdout
remains valid for what it was built to test, which is card shape.
